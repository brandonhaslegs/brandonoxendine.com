Future Cash

An app that shows your bank balance on every day in the future. I designed and built the whole thing.

Role: Visual Design, Branding, Code, Website
Live at myfuture.cash
Get it on the App Store

Every money app I’ve tried is complicated af and almost always only deals with the past: what you spent last month, a pie chart of your coffee habit. Cool… but what I actually want to know is whether I’m gonna be okay next Tuesday, or if I can afford a trip in March.

In Future Cash you input your balance, bills, paychecks and anything you’ve got planned (a new laptop, a trip), and it shows you what your account will look like on every day in the future. Bills and paydays repeat on their own, so mostly you just update your balance.

The forecast

The main screen is a chart of your balance for the next month, or two, or three, six or twelve. Paydays, big purchases and trips are marked as points on the line so you can see what's moving your balance from day to day.

Below that is every payment in order, with what’s left after each one. Trips get grouped together with a little bar.

Up top there are two numbers: balance and safe to spend (how much you could spend right now without going below zero at any point in the next year).

Can I afford it?

Most money decisions are really just one question, so it gets its own screen with a big dial. Spin it to a price and Future Cash tells you the first day you can buy it without ever dipping below zero.

At a glance

Most days you only need two numbers, which you can easily add as widgets on your home screen. You can also ask Siri what’s safe to spend, or tell it your new balance.

Notifications

A forecast is only as good as the balance you gave it, so the app bugs you a little (in a nice way). It tells you when a bill’s coming, asks if your paycheck landed, checks whether you actually bought the thing you planned, and gives you a heads up a month before the holidays where you live so you can plan a trip. Tapping one takes you straight to the thing it’s about.

I wanted them to sound like a friend who’s good with money, not a bank. Every one of them gets a switch in Settings, and they’re only on for your iPhone by default, so your Mac and iPad don’t buzz you about the same thing twice.

Private by design

Future Cash never connects to your bank. Handing an app your bank login is the sketchiest part of most finance apps, and a forecast doesn’t even need it. Your balance and your plan are enough. Everything lives on your devices and in your own iCloud, which keeps your iPhone, iPad and Mac in sync. No accounts, no servers, no analytics.

The app

It’s a real native app on each device, not one design stretched across all three. iPhone and iPad get tabs, the Mac gets a sidebar and keyboard shortcuts.

I didn’t want adding stuff to feel like filling out a form, so every sheet is custom: the amount big at the top, details on tinted cards, soft buttons. Trips look like boarding passes. And the first time you open it, it only asks for three things (your balance, your paycheck and your bills) and you’re done.

Free to try for a month, then you pay 4.99 once to unlock it. No subscription, no ads.

Identity and website

With a name like Future Cash, the icon kind of designed itself: we're looking into the future, into a crystal ball. The same periwinkle became the accent color for the whole app.

The website has the same vibe: a night sky full of nebulas and drifting stars behind the crystal ball. The features are tiny live versions of the app instead of screenshots, and the dial on the page actually runs the app’s forecast on a made-up plan, so you can play with it.
