A system for categories

A rental catalog is wide and shallow: thousands of products in a handful of families that people browse by. So categories became the backbone of the visual system. Each of the ten top-level categories got its own color, an icon for itself and one for each subcategory, and those followed the category everywhere: the menu, the category page title, the filter controls, the product tiles.

The menu was where it came together. It’s a two-layer dropdown where each row lights up in its category’s color. In the implementation we also drew an invisible triangle from the cursor to the subcategory list, so you can move diagonally to the second layer without accidentally switching categories on the way. Nobody notices it, which is the point.

Icons

I drew 44 icons, one for every category and subcategory, on a 24px grid with heavy lineweight, used in the menu, on category pages and across the product. Each top-level family carried its category color.

Foundations

Underneath the category system sat a small set of foundations: Grover’s pink, a near-black text color, a single hairline color for borders, two shadows and a short list of corner radii.

Product discovery

To give users a new way to discover products and keep the experience fresh and automatically updated, we built a product feed that shows something new each time the page is visited. Rules like “Show the top product from the top 4 categories in the first row” satisfy business needs while also showing users the hottest products right now. Rules like “Show the top products in a random category” enable discovery of new products that users might not know we have. “Display a random collection” lets users dive deeper into our curated content.

Product page

A lot of ad traffic is sent directly to the product detail pages, so on these pages we had the unique challenge of showing all the product details in addition to explaining how Grover works. The result is a long page with tons of information on it, structured in a way that answers new users’ questions about Grover.
